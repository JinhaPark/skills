# Evidence, operating forecasts and normalized cash flow

## Sources and comparability
Prioritize filings and primary evidence. For US companies use SEC filings; for
others use local regulatory and exchange disclosures. Cross-check IR earnings
releases, supplementary schedules, conference calls, proxy statements and
compensation disclosures. Read the actual tables and notes supporting segment
revenue/profitability, cash flows, debt, leases, stock compensation, buybacks and
share counts. Do not build a model from search-result summaries.

State price date/time/timezone, exchange, currency, live-price versus close,
latest reporting period and filing date, fiscal-year end month and FX date.
Align currencies and units and apply ADR conversion ratios. Distinguish current
shares used for market capitalization, weighted-average diluted shares used for
EPS, and forecast diluted shares used for valuation.

Place direct source links, document titles, periods and table/section locations
near material facts and figures. Separate facts, management explanations,
guidance, dated and sourced market consensus, own estimates and unverified
hypotheses. Never label an own estimate as consensus when consensus is
unavailable. Resolve period, definition and measurement-basis differences before
interpreting discrepancies. Mark paid or inaccessible data NA; identify any
alternative metric by its real name and limitations, never as a substitute CDS.
Use source/claim/metric identifiers and the [handoff contract](handoff-contract.md)
to preserve traceability through subsequent modeling and writing.

## Connect business evidence to the forecast
Explain products/services, purchasing customers, reasons to buy, channels,
recurring revenue, supply chain and competition. Show the full economic link:
verified business facts → segment volumes/prices or appropriate operating drivers
→ revenue → costs and operating margin → after-tax earnings and diluted EPS →
working capital, investment and cash flow → enterprise and equity value.

Where volume and average selling price are undisclosed or inappropriate, use
actual economic drivers such as customers, utilization, contract value, seats,
ARPU, stores or transaction volume. Do not present reverse-engineered undisclosed
quantities or prices as confirmed facts. Quantify where supportable the effects
of price, volume, mix, utilization, materials, labor, customer concentration and
competitor pricing on revenue and margins.

For the detailed company model, compare at least three historical fiscal years,
the latest TTM, and three forecast fiscal years in the same table. Include revenue
and growth, segment results, operating profit/margin, tax rate, diluted EPS, CFO,
cash capital expenditure, normalized FCF, net debt and diluted shares.

Calculate TTM reproducibly, for example latest annual + current YTD − prior-year
comparable YTD, without overlapping periods. Do not mix adjusted and accounting
EPS definitions. Where required history is missing, identify the extent, reason
and effect on comparability.

After the forecast table, explain the evidence and magnitude of major assumption
changes. Quantify and explain differences from guidance and accessible consensus.
Do not apply a common growth rate to every segment or mechanically extrapolate
historical averages. Distinguish cycle peaks/troughs, price increases, inventory
normalization, new capacity and restructuring lags. Three forecast years are a
minimum reporting range, not a restriction on the DCF's explicit forecast and
transition-to-stability periods; choose those periods for the business.

## Normalize FCF and assess capital allocation
Start from the cash flow statement and show a bridge:
reported CFO → nonrecurring-item and working-capital normalization → cash
investment deductions → normalized FCF. Display company-defined and own-defined
FCF side by side and explain differences.

Examine as material gross capex before asset-disposal proceeds, intangible and
software investment, lease cash outflows, acquisition costs, restructuring,
pensions and temporary tax effects. Do not repeatedly remove recurring
'one-off' costs or split maintenance and growth capex with unsupported precision.

Missing gross capex does not automatically make all enterprise valuation NA.
Use the most appropriate disclosed investment measure, explaining net/gross
differences, omitted items, estimation method, error direction and materiality.
If a material gap prevents even a reasonable range, withhold the affected
valuation while distinguishing methods that remain feasible.

Stock compensation is not free because it is added back in CFO. Apply an
appropriate economic-cost or expected-dilution treatment consistently with the
model, checking for double counting between cash flows and equity conversion.
Buybacks do not increase operating FCF. Distinguish purchase price/size, net
share reduction, compensation dilution and debt funding. Judge dividend and
buyback sustainability from normalized FCF after maturities and necessary
investment.

Reconcile screening FCF derived from CFO to valuation FCFF or FCFE, explicitly
identifying interest, tax, lease, investment and financing treatment. Preserve
validated negative cash flows and distinguish missing data from a real zero.
Follow [valuation policy](valuation-policy.md) for the valuation and distress
model rather than silently replacing unavailable inputs.
